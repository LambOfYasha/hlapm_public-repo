# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

This repository teaches human-led AI practice. AI may assist with research, organization, drafting, implementation, review, and verification, but should not silently erase human responsibility or create the appearance of mastery where it has not been demonstrated.

## Core Rules

1. Prioritize human understanding, not merely successful AI output.
2. Expose important assumptions, dependencies, constraints, and verification requirements.
3. Distinguish what AI performed from what the learner demonstrated.
4. Encourage independent verification when consequences matter.
5. Preserve useful failures and corrections instead of hiding them.
6. Avoid unnecessary dependency on one model, interface, vendor, or prompt formula.
7. Gradually transfer appropriate responsibility from AI assistance toward human reproduction, adaptation, diagnosis, and teaching.
8. Prefer authentic project work for assessments when meaningful real work exists.
9. Prefer authentic project extensions for challenges rather than artificial difficulty.
10. Never treat polished AI output alone as evidence of mastery.

## Assessment Rule

A live-project assessment should identify the capability being taught, produce meaningful project value, define learner-owned work and permitted AI help, use observable success criteria, require relevant evidence, preserve untested dimensions as untested, and record learning debt separately from project completion.

## Challenge Progression Rule

A full challenge follows demonstrated assessment evidence and requires:

1. a learner-authored advanced prompt built from acquired knowledge;
2. a finished real-project deliverable;
3. an explicit fair but strict deadline;
4. acceptance criteria and verification;
5. separate evaluation of knowledge/prompt mastery, product/application mastery, and workflow/deadline discipline.

AI should not write the learner's final advanced prompt or silently complete the product when those are the capabilities under evaluation.

## Status Vocabulary

Use clear status labels when useful:

- `EXPERIMENT`
- `DRAFT`
- `REVIEW`
- `PROPOSED`
- `APPROVED`
- `CURRENT`
- `DEPRECATED`
- `ARCHIVED`

Do not present experiments as established standards.

## Public Repository Guardrails

Do not commit:

- credentials, secrets, or authentication material;
- private learner or employee information;
- confidential client or organizational material;
- proprietary source material without permission;
- private project canon or unreleased intellectual property;
- content whose publication rights are unclear.

Use sanitized, generalized examples whenever real private project experience is converted into public teaching material.

## Human Authority

AI may propose, explain, compare, draft, implement approved work, and help verify. Humans remain responsible for publication, consequential decisions, professional claims, employment decisions, licensing choices, privacy decisions, and final judgments about whether evidence supports mastery.
