# Contributing

Human-Led AI Project Mastery should not become a prompt dump. Contributions should help people become more capable while using AI, not merely collect impressive outputs.

## Useful Contributions

Examples include:

- teaching workflows;
- live-project assessments;
- deadline-bound challenges;
- prompt-design methods;
- verification strategies;
- failure analyses;
- learning-debt methods;
- workplace-training adaptations;
- case studies;
- accessibility improvements;
- workflow improvements;
- documentation corrections.

## Prompt Contributions

A reusable prompt should explain, when relevant:

- what knowledge supports it;
- why major instructions exist;
- assumptions and dependencies;
- human responsibilities;
- AI responsibilities;
- expected deliverable;
- verification method;
- common failure modes;
- how a learner could eventually construct or adapt their own version.

## Evidence Over Impression

When contributing an assessment, challenge, case study, or mastery claim, distinguish:

- AI-generated work;
- learner-observed work;
- learner-explained work;
- learner-reproduced work;
- learner-adapted work;
- learner-verified work;
- independently executed work.

Do not inflate mastery because the final artifact looks polished.

## Protect People and Private Work

Do not submit secrets, private learner records, confidential employer/client material, unreleased intellectual property, or personally identifying information without appropriate rights and consent. Sanitize case studies where possible.

## Keep Changes Reviewable

Prefer focused contributions with clear intent. Explain what problem is being solved, why the change helps learners, and what evidence or reasoning supports it.

## Licensing

The repository's final licensing model is still under review. Contributions may be subject to additional contribution/licensing guidance before broad external contribution is encouraged.
