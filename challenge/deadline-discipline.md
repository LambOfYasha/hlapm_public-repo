# Deadline Discipline

Deadline discipline exists to develop professional reliability, not stress for its own sake.

## A Fair but Strict Deadline

A challenge deadline should account for:

- demonstrated mastery;
- task scope and complexity;
- research and setup;
- dependencies and tool availability;
- testing and verification;
- review and correction time;
- known accessibility or project constraints.

Record an explicit start time, exact deadline, and timezone.

**Strict means explicit, not arbitrary.**

## Legitimate Adjustment Conditions

A deadline should not be silently moved. A revision may be appropriate when a meaningful reason changes the original challenge contract, such as:

- material scope change;
- verified external blocker;
- required dependency unavailable;
- service or tool outage;
- safety requirement;
- required external review;
- accessibility requirement;
- another material condition outside the original contract.

Record the reason and revised deadline explicitly.

## Separate Knowledge From Time Management

A learner can demonstrate strong knowledge while underestimating testing time. Conversely, a learner can submit an AI-generated artifact on time while demonstrating little personal understanding.

Therefore deadline performance contributes to **workflow discipline**, not to a hidden all-purpose mastery score.

## Health and Safety

Do not encourage sleep deprivation, unsafe rushing, skipped testing, skipped security review, concealed errors, or unhealthy working practices simply to preserve a challenge deadline. Responsible completion is part of professional discipline.
