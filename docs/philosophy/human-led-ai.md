# Human-Led AI

## Core Idea

> **AI should increase what a person can accomplish without requiring that person to understand less.**

Human-led AI is not anti-automation and it is not a requirement that every person independently master every task AI touches. It is a framework for making dependency, judgment, verification, and learning intentional.

## Capability, Not Performance Theater

A polished result can hide weak understanding. A learner may obtain excellent code, research, writing, planning, or analysis while remaining unable to explain the assumptions, recognize failure, adapt the result, or verify whether it is correct.

Human-Led AI Project Mastery therefore distinguishes:

- what the AI can do;
- what the project accomplished;
- what the learner can demonstrate;
- what remains untested;
- what still requires AI support.

## Prompt Engineering From Knowledge

Prompt design should mature as domain understanding improves. The learner should gradually become better able to decide what context matters, which constraints are necessary, which assumptions are risky, what the AI should own, what the human must retain, and how the result will be verified.

The goal is not prompt complexity for its own sake.

## Verification as a Skill

AI-assisted work becomes more dependable when the learner can identify what deserves verification and choose an appropriate method: tests, source checking, calculations, peer review, professional review, comparison, or direct inspection.

## Learning Debt

Learning debt is the gap between what the project required and what the learner can personally explain, reproduce, adapt, diagnose, verify, or teach.

Learning debt is not automatically bad. Some dependency is rational. The important task is to make meaningful gaps visible and prioritize them according to risk and future value.

## Authentic Project Learning

Real projects are preferred when practical because they force the learner to deal with incomplete information, tradeoffs, dependencies, quality requirements, and consequences. Assessment and challenge systems should therefore advance real work rather than exist only as demonstrations.

## Long-Term Direction

The intended progression is:

`Learn → Practice → Assess → Challenge → Independent Practice → Teach → Systematize`

This progression is recursive. New tools, projects, and responsibilities can expose new learning debt at any level.
